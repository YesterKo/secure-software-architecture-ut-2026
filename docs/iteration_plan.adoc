= Iteration Plan

[cols="1,3,3,2,4", options="header"]
|===
| Phase
| Focus
| Main artifact
| Risks addressed
| Evidence of risk reduction

| 1
| *System boundaries, identity, and access control*

Clarify what data and responsibilities belong to the portal, how users are authenticated, and how authorization and delegation work across agencies.
| Data ownership and trust-boundary sketch, together with an initial identity and delegation model.
| R-001, R-004, R-005
| Major data categories have an identified authoritative source.

Representative citizen, organization, and delegation scenarios can be explained using the access-control model.

Authentication behavior is defined for normal operation as well as identity-provider failures or revoked credentials.

| 2
| *External integrations and distributed workflows*

Determine how the portal communicates with agency systems and how workflows behave when external systems fail, change, or only partially complete an operation.
| Integration and failure-handling model for representative agency workflows.
| R-002, R-006, R-007, R-008
| Representative integrations cover both modern and legacy-style systems.

Behavior is defined for unavailable or slow external systems.

A multi-system workflow can recover from partial failure without losing or duplicating a request.

Interface changes and versioning can be handled without requiring unrelated parts of the portal to change.

| 3
| *Auditability and privacy*

Define what important actions must be traceable and how sensitive information is handled when it crosses system boundaries.
| Audit and privacy model covering important events, data handling, and notifications.
| R-003, R-004, R-010
| Important citizen and administrator actions can be reconstructed from the planned audit information.

Ownership, access, retention, and integrity expectations for audit data are documented.

Notification rules show that sensitive details remain inside appropriately protected channels.

| 4
| *Availability, scaling, and resilience*

Check whether the architecture can remain usable during high demand and when dependencies become slow or unavailable.
| Workload and resilience model for architecture-critical workflows.
| R-002, R-006, R-009
| Normal and peak-load assumptions are documented.

Likely bottlenecks and external capacity limits have been identified.

The expected behavior under overload and dependency failure is defined for critical workflows.

The architecture can explain how unrelated services remain usable when one dependency or workflow fails.
|===
