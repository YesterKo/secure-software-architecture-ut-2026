= Architectural Drivers
:toc:
:toclevels: 2

== Mission goals

* Provide citizens with one trusted digital entry point for selected national-defence services instead of relying on separate agency-specific systems.
* Protect citizens' rights and public trust by ensuring that identity, health, and defence-related personal data are handled responsibly and lawfully.
* Give citizens accurate, authoritative, and up-to-date information about their defence obligations, requests, and decisions.
* Make conscription, health-assessment, and reserve-service workflows easier to access and complete.
* Keep citizens informed about summonses, case status changes, document requests, decisions, and actions they need to take.
* Improve cooperation between KRA, the Estonian Defence Forces, and health-information systems to reduce manual work and delays.
* Reduce the long-term cost and effort required to operate, maintain, and extend the portal and its integrations.
* Apply service-specific authorization and delegation rules so that actions can only be performed by the citizen or an authorized representative where delegation is permitted.


== Stakeholders

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Stakeholder |Need or concern

|Residents/citizens |Access defence-related services easily; understand their obligations, requests, and decisions; trust that sensitive data is protected; receive timely notifications.
|Service provider organizations/agencies |KRA and the Estonian Defence Forces need reliable integrations, clear responsibility boundaries, secure access to required data, and traceable actions.
|Administrators/helpdesk |Tools for troubleshooting user issues; visibility into system health; ability to inspect audit logs; safe ways to assist users without violating privacy.
|Auditors/oversight |Complete, immutable audit trails; transparency features; evidence of compliance with regulations; ability to trace data flows and decisions.
|External registries/backend systems |KVKR, TIS, and Estonian Defence Forces systems need authenticated access, controlled request and retry rates, clear interface contracts, and defined failure handling.
|Operations/security teams |Observability, monitoring, and alerting; secure deployment model; incident response workflows; clear trust boundaries; ability to enforce policies and detect anomalies.
|===

== Assumptions

* National digital identity (eID / OIDC provider) will remain stable and available with high uptime.
* Required machine-to-machine interfaces to KVKR, Estonian Defence Forces systems, and TIS will be available and documented.
* KVKR, Estonian Defence Forces systems, and TIS remain the authoritative sources for the data they own.
* Citizens primarily access the portal via modern browsers and mobile devices.
* Document upload sizes and formats follow existing government guidelines.
* Notification channels (SMS, email) are provided by trusted third-party services with SLAs (Service Level Agreements)
* The portal will not replace KVKR, TIS, or Estonian Defence Forces backend systems. It integrates with them and does not become the authoritative source for their domain data.
* Delegation rules are service-specific and may restrict or prohibit delegation for national-defence services.
* The system will operate in a government-managed cloud or data center environment.
* All participating agencies accept the portal’s audit and logging requirements.

== Open questions

* Which data must the portal store itself, beyond portal-owned data such as preferences, notifications, audit records, and temporary workflow state?
* Which national-defence operations, if any, may be delegated to another citizen?
* How will document retention requirements differ between KRA, the Estonian Defence Forces, and health-related workflows?
* What level of audit information may be shown to citizens?
* How should the portal behave when KVKR, TIS, or a Estonian Defence Forces system is unavailable: queue work, show partial information, or stop the affected workflow?
* Which workflows must be synchronous (real-time) versus asynchronous (event-driven)?
* What is the expected peak load during conscription call-up periods, reserve exercises, or other high-demand events?
* How will authorization rules for KRA and Estonian Defence Forces staff be represented and enforced?
* Do all three external systems provide interfaces suitable for the required workflows, or will integration-specific adaptation be necessary?
* What are the long-term expectations for onboarding new agencies or services?

== Out of scope

* Public services unrelated to the selected national-defence service domain.
* Replacing or rebuilding KVKR, TIS, or Estonian Defence Forces backend systems.
* Defining or changing legal rules governing military obligations, health assessment, identity, authorization, delegation, or data retention.
* Implementing or operating external systems such as the national identity provider, TIS, notification providers, or agency backend systems.
* Redesigning the internal processes used by KRA, the Estonian Defence Forces, or healthcare organizations after responsibility for a request has passed to them.
* Providing non-digital service channels such as in-person, telephone, or paper-based government services.
