== Mission goals

List 5-8 goals that explain what the Citizen Services Portal should achieve.

* Provide a unified digital entry point for citizens to access government services through a single, consistent interface instead of fragmented agency-specific portals.
* Ensure confidentiality and privacy of personal data, enforcing strict access controls, data minimization, and transparency so citizens understand how their data is used.
* Deliver accurate, authoritative, and up‑to‑date information by integrating securely with official registries and agency back-end systems.
* Offer a convenient, intuitive user experience that reduces friction in accessing services, submitting cases, and receiving updates.
* Enable timely and reliable notifications (email, SMS, push, inbox) for events such as case status changes, document requests, or security alerts.
* Improve interoperability between agencies, enabling controlled, auditable data exchange that reduces manual work and accelerates service delivery.
* Reduce operational and maintenance costs compared to legacy systems through modern architecture, automation, and clearer separation of responsibilities.
* Support secure digital identity and delegation, allowing citizens to act on behalf of dependents or organizations when authorized.

== Stakeholders

Identify stakeholders and what each needs from the architecture.

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Stakeholder |Need or concern

|Residents/citizens |Access services easily; trust that their data is protected; understand how their data is used; receive timely notifications; complete workflows without technical issues.
|Service provider organizations/agencies |Reliable, secure access to citizen data; clear API contracts; predictable integration patterns; auditability; minimal operational overhead.
|Administrators/helpdesk |Tools for troubleshooting user issues; visibility into system health; ability to inspect audit logs; safe ways to assist users without violating privacy.
|Auditors/oversight |Complete, immutable audit trails; transparency features; evidence of compliance with regulations; ability to trace data flows and decisions.
|External registries/back-end systems |Predictable integration load; secure and authenticated access; clear failure-handling expectations; minimal disruption to existing systems.
|Operations/security teams |Observability, monitoring, and alerting; secure deployment model; incident response workflows; clear trust boundaries; ability to enforce policies and detect anomalies.
|===

== Quality attributes and constraints

List 8-12 quality attributes or constraints.

[cols="1,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|ID |Driver |Why it matters

|QA‑001 - Security & Privacy |The system handles sensitive personal data and must comply with national regulations (GDPR, sector‑specific laws). |Breaches or misuse of citizen data would cause legal violations, loss of public trust, and potential harm to individuals. Security is the primary architectural driver for all components.
|QA‑002 - Availability |Citizens and agencies rely on the portal for critical services; downtime directly impacts public trust and service delivery. |Government services must remain accessible even during peak periods or emergencies. Outages disrupt essential workflows and damage credibility.
|QA‑003 - Auditability |Every access, change, and data exchange must be traceable for compliance and oversight. |Without strong audit trails, the system cannot demonstrate lawful processing, detect misuse, or support investigations. Auditors require immutable, complete records.
|QA‑004 - Interoperability |The portal must integrate with multiple external systems using stable, well‑defined interfaces. |Agencies use different technologies and legacy systems. Poor interoperability leads to fragile integrations, inconsistent data, and increased operational burden.
|QA‑005 - Scalability |Usage may spike during elections, tax season, or emergencies; the system must scale without degradation. |Sudden load increases must not cause slowdowns or failures. Scalability ensures predictable performance and prevents service interruptions during national events.
|QA‑006 - Usability |Citizens vary widely in digital literacy; poor UX leads to support burden and reduced adoption. |If the portal is confusing or frustrating, citizens will avoid it or overwhelm helpdesk teams. Usability directly affects adoption and satisfaction.
|QA‑007 - Reliability & Fault Tolerance |External systems may fail; the portal must degrade gracefully and avoid data loss. |Many workflows depend on external registries. The portal must handle partial failures without corrupting data or blocking unrelated services.
|QA‑008 - Maintainability |The system must evolve as new services and agencies join; architecture should minimize coupling and simplify onboarding. |Government services change frequently. High maintainability reduces long‑term costs and enables continuous improvement without major rewrites.
|QA‑009 - Transparency |Citizens must understand how their data is accessed and by whom; transparency builds trust. |Lack of transparency leads to suspicion and complaints. Clear visibility into data usage is essential for compliance and citizen confidence.
|QA‑010 - Regulatory Compliance |Must adhere to national digital identity standards, data retention rules, and public‑sector security baselines. |Non‑compliance results in legal penalties, forced shutdowns, and mandatory redesigns. Compliance shapes many architectural constraints.
|QA‑011 - Performance |Core workflows (login, case submission, document upload) must respond quickly to avoid frustration. |Slow interactions reduce usability and increase abandonment rates. Performance is essential for citizen satisfaction and operational efficiency.
|QA‑012 - Cost Constraints |Public‑sector budgets require efficient resource usage and predictable operational costs. |Architecture must avoid unnecessary complexity and expensive technologies. Cost efficiency ensures long‑term sustainability and political acceptance.
|===

== Assumptions

List assumptions that may later need validation.

* National digital identity (eID / OIDC provider) will remain stable and available with high uptime.
* External registries provide reliable APIs with documented schemas.
* Agencies agree on standardized data exchange formats.
* Citizens primarily access the portal via modern browsers and mobile devices.
* Document upload sizes and formats follow existing government guidelines.
* Notification channels (SMS, email) are provided by trusted third-party services with SLAs (Service Level Agreements)
* The portal will not replace agency-specific back-end systems, only integrate with them.
* Delegation rules (acting on behalf of dependents/organizations) follow existing legal frameworks.
* The system will operate in a government-managed cloud or data center environment.
* All participating agencies accept the portal’s audit and logging requirements.

== Open questions

List open questions as questions, not as hidden assumptions.

* What is the exact system boundary: does the portal store citizen data or only proxy it?
* Which agency is responsible for identity lifecycle events (revocation, delegation changes)?
* How will document retention policies differ across agencies?
* What level of transparency is legally required for audit log visibility to citizens?
* How will the portal behave when an external registry is unavailable: queue requests, show partial data, or block workflows?
* Which workflows must be synchronous (real-time) versus asynchronous (event-driven)?
* What is the expected peak load during national events (elections, tax deadlines)?
* How will cross-agency authorization rules be defined and enforced?
* Are there legacy systems that cannot support modern API standards?
* What are the long-term expectations for onboarding new agencies or services?
